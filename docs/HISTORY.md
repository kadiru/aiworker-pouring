# Al-Jazari: evidence for the story

Checked October 28, 2026. Use “Al-Jazari” consistently in captions; “El-Jazari” is an alternate spelling in the project brief. These sources establish historical designs/manuscript evidence, not an unbroken technical lineage to this robot.

| Claim or object | Evidence and precise use |
| --- | --- |
| An automaton pours wine | The David Collection's inventory **20/1988** is a 1315 manuscript leaf showing a bottle pouring into a beaker, which the figure then raises to its lips. Its catalog describes eight cycles per hour and places Al-Jazari's writing in the early thirteenth century. This is the strongest direct visual/story connection to bottle-to-cup pouring. [Original museum PDF](https://catalog.davidmus.dk/en/item/1542/20-1988.pdf) |
| A wine-serving cup | The Met's **55.121.14**, dated **1315**, depicts a goblet whose bird whistles around filling and drinking. It is a different device from the bottle-holding figure. Do not present it as an illustration of the same mechanism. [Museum record](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/451301) |
| Manuscript date versus invention date | The two leaves above are later copies. Caption them “1315 manuscript copy of Al-Jazari's work,” not “robot built in 1315” or an original 1206 drawing. The David catalog supports the earlier authorship; the objects' catalog dates describe surviving leaves. |
| Programmable musicians | Nadia Ambrosetti's University of Milan dissertation (2009/2010), printed p.113 / PDF page 113, reports Noel Sharkey's interpretation of rearranged pegs controlling the boat musicians' rhythm. This is an interpretation of musical mechanisms, not evidence that the wine-pouring figure was programmable. [Dissertation](https://air.unimi.it/retrieve/dfa8b98f-4eaf-748b-e053-3a05fe0a3a96/phd_unimi_R07642.pdf#page=113) |
| Why omit “first programmable humanoid” | Michael Krzyzaniak's Arizona State University dissertation (2016), §2.2.5, challenges programmability claims: his reading of the text finds fixed peg arrangements but no stated reconfiguration. The disagreement prevents treating a “first” claim as settled. [Author's chapter](https://michaelkrzyzaniak.com/Research/Dissertation/chapter_02.php), [dissertation PDF](https://michaelkrzyzaniak.com/Research/Dissertation.pdf) |

The musical boat, the water clock with drummers, the wine-serving goblet and the bottle-pouring figure must remain distinct in editing and narration. Their shared association with Al-Jazari is not permission to transfer one device's mechanism to another. Secondary scholarship also differs in chapter numbering; identify objects by description and museum inventory, not an unverified chapter label.

## Supported wording

“More than eight centuries ago, Al-Jazari described ingenious mechanical devices, including automata associated with pouring and serving drinks. This experiment revisits the gesture with a modern robot.”

“Water flow, electric current and learned control offer a way to think about how people turn an intention into an artifact.” This is our analogy. Water can carry energy and participate in timing/control in a mechanism; electric current powers actuators; a learned policy computes actions. AI is not a third physical current, and these are not three sequential replacements in a proven historical chain.

Avoid: “Al-Jazari invented AI,” “the first programmable humanoid poured drinks,” “all modern robots descend from this machine,” and an unexplained cut from teleoperation to a claim that the robot learned. A modern robot's humanoid appearance says nothing about its control mode.
